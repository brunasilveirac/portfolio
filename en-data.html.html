01

Data lake · Marketing

A funnel dashboard that refreshes every 15 minutes

The team needed paid, organic and lead generation performance in one place, as close to real time as possible. The data lived in Meta Ads, Instagram, landing pages and the CRM, each with its own dashboard.

Before

Four tools, four reports, manual reconciliation.

→
After

Layered warehouse and a single dashboard with custom date ranges and a campaign selector.

My role: product and request owner, defining what the dashboard must answer and validating deliveries, with technical execution shared with the head of technology and AI coding assistants.

Behind the delivery +

How it was built

  • Postgres in four layers (raw, core, ops, marts). Only six aggregated views are exposed to the front end.
  • Row-level security and grants verified: the public key cannot read base tables.
  • n8n ingestion with transactional begin, commit and fail calls, leases and a per-run audit log, with no duplicated data.
  • Instagram backfill of 91 days with no gaps and 272 posts loaded.
  • Leads attributed by UTM and landing page, with MQL scoring by profile (A to E) and a conversion event sent to Meta only for the hottest profiles.

Follow-up

  • The same architecture was replicated for a consortium finance client, with HubSpot and YouTube: 338 of 359 deals now have an identified source in the sales funnel.
02

Customer base · Automotive retail

1,020 oil changes across six spreadsheets become an actionable customer base

A chain of six gas stations did 1,000 to 1,500 oil changes a month, each station logging them its own way. Nobody could tell who came back, who was satisfied or why a customer stopped coming.

Before

Six tabs, 96 empty phone numbers, no license plates, no return date.

→
After

Clean base, phone numbers in international format and a target return date for every oil change.

My role: data profiling and cleansing, segmentation of customers with more than six months since their last change, and the KPIs for the management dashboard.

Behind the delivery +

How it was built

  • Spreadsheet audit: 1,020 rows across six tabs, with gaps mapped field by field.
  • Single table keyed by phone and change date, with the return date generated automatically (change + 6 months).
  • Win-back segment of about 600 customers who had not returned in over six months.
  • A fuel coupon proposed as the way to attribute each return to the message that drove it.

Dashboard KPIs

  • Six-month return rate, by station.
  • Post-service NPS, by band (promoter, passive, detractor).
  • Churn reasons.
  • Revenue coverage.

03

Reporting · Sports events

A broadcast to 2,674 leads with its cost forecast before hitting send

A marathon with a goal of 2,000 registrations needed to know what each messaging round cost and what it returned. Every broadcast now ships with a forecast and a report.

Before

Broadcasts with no cost estimate and results known only afterwards.

→
After

Cost and send time forecast, same-day report and the next round proposed from the numbers.

My role: running the broadcasts, cost forecasting, reporting and recommending the next rounds.

Behind the delivery +

Case numbers

  • A round to 2,674 leads with an estimated cost of R$ 770 and about three hours of sending.
  • With 76 spots left, three broadcasts to non-registered leads proposed at an estimated R$ 500.
  • 1,537 tickets sold at the last recorded measurement.

The same method elsewhere

  • Per-message cost surveyed before launching a retail client's sequence: R$ 0.03 to R$ 0.06 per utility message.
  • Broadcast reports for an event series held in several cities.
