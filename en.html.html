01

Automation · WhatsApp

A WhatsApp sequence that asks, reminds and warns on its own

A chain of six gas stations wanted to bring back oil change customers and know whether they left satisfied. The official number was blocking the timeline and each station tracked services in its own spreadsheet.

Before

No follow-up after the service and no satisfaction measure.

→
After

A survey right after the service, a month-five reminder with an offer and an overdue-oil notice in month six.

My role: message cost forecasting, number activation with Meta, copywriting, send schedules and running the broadcasts.

Behind the delivery +

How it was built

  • The physical number swapped for a virtual one to unblock the timeline, with business verification on Meta.
  • Survey sent every Monday morning to the week's customers, with three n8n workflows ready for the full sequence: daily load, reminder with up to three retries and opt-out, and central sending through Chatwoot.
  • Satisfaction survey built as a fixed state machine, with no AI. Low scores get a tag and go to a human agent.
  • Daily batches for a partner base of 20 to 40 thousand numbers, below the account limit, to protect the number's reputation.

First result

  • First survey sent to 49 customers: 12 completed it, none with a score from 1 to 6.
  • Utility messages at R$ 0.03 to R$ 0.06, surveyed before switching the sequence on.

02

Product · CRM

A custom CRM, specified with AI and shipped in sprints

The same client needed to log oil changes by station and track who comes back. Instead of adapting an off-the-shelf tool, the CRM was designed from scratch around the managers' routine.

Before

Spreadsheets per station and broadcasts assembled by hand from them.

→
After

Pipelines for new customers, post-service and win-back, per-station access and an API that feeds the broadcasts.

My role: requirements with the client, sprint prioritization and acceptance, and the API that connects the CRM to automated broadcasts.

Behind the delivery +

How it was built

  • A 431-line product requirements document: configurable groups and pipelines, admin and operator roles, import with preview and deduplication, change history.
  • Each feature got a spec, design and task list generated with AI before any code, with implementation assisted by Claude models.
  • Sprints in ClickUp, with the CRM stabilized before automating the sends.
  • Oil price and spec fields plus date-filtered export, requested by operations.

Decision discipline

  • 64 decisions logged in the project, each with context, alternatives and consequences.
  • Training for the station managers planned alongside the delivery.

03

AI · Sales agents

WhatsApp sales agents that clients learn to tune themselves

AI agents handle leads on WhatsApp for several clients. Every change in tone or offer depended on the tech team, and the agency's own agent tried to sell everything at once.

Before

A broad-scope agent and every prompt change in the team's queue.

→
After

An agent focused on a single product and clients trained to edit their own prompts.

My role: prompt engineering, QA tests on the agents before each release, client onboarding and testing of the internal agent platform.

Behind the delivery +

How it was built

  • Agent scope narrowed to one product, with the prompt rewritten after each feedback round.
  • Prompt QA tests: conversation scenarios with expected outputs, run to validate behavior, guardrails and every change before deploy.
  • Onboarding of fashion and other clients so they can edit prompts on their own.
  • Number and Meta business account set up for new agents.
  • Usability suggestions for the agent platform, generated with a UX skill and adopted by development.

AI in the team's daily work

  • A Markdown knowledge base of all clients, connected to Claude Code, kept up to date from meeting notes and chats.
  • A nightly AI summary of client WhatsApp groups, versioned on GitHub, with no personal lead data.

Two voices · Side by side

Fixed flow or AI agent.

Not every conversation needs AI. This is the rule I use to decide.

Voice 1

Fixed flow

  • WhenThe question is closed and the answer fits in a number or a button.
  • CostOnly the WhatsApp message.
  • RiskNo answer outside the script.
  • ExitA tag and an alert to a human when something unexpected happens.
  • ExampleSatisfaction survey after an oil change.

Voice 2

AI agent

  • WhenThe conversation is open, with questions, objections and negotiation.
  • CostThe message plus model usage on every reply.
  • RiskNeeds a clear scope, guardrails in the prompt and QA tests before release.
  • ExitHandoff to the sales team when the lead is ready.
  • ExampleAn agent that sells one product and qualifies leads on WhatsApp.
